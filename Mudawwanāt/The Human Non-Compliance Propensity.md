We are more often inclined to the worldly pleasure more than it is to the Hereafter despite the persistent warnings and promises of the Qur'an.
It's indeed in our nature. Beautifully depicted by this metaphor by [[Marājiʿ/Mufakkirūn/Imām Ibn al-Jawzī|Imām Ibn al-Jawzī]]:

> [!note] Page [69](zotero://open/library/items/FYSA9UFZ?annotation=L2NIQG75&page=69)
>
> the example of the innate nature of humans and its inclination to this worldly life is like running water that is always befitting to travel downward because going upward requires (strenuous) exertion.
>
> لأن مثل الطبع في ميله إلى الدنيا كالماء الجاري، فإنه يطلب الهبوط؛ وإنما رفعه إلى فوق يحتاج إلى التكلف.

And it is for this prevalent and pervasive nature that it's **less foreseeable** for the human to comply to the Qur'an than it is for the latter to Qur'an the human.

> [!note] Page [70](zotero://open/library/items/FYSA9UFZ?annotation=QF9JUA4U&page=70)
>
> Rather, it is indeed more of a surprise ('ajab) to find the innate nature of humans succeeding in overcoming these attracdons and thereby becoming tamed and controlled!
>
> «صيد الخاطر» (ص25):  
> «فأما الطبع، فجواذبه كثيرة، **وليس العجب أن يغلب، إنما العجب أن يغلب.**»
